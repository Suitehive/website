**What you'll learn:** honest answers to the questions people ask most before trying Suitehive.

## Is Suitehive production-ready?

No. Suitehive is in early development. Mail, Calendar, Auth and the admin console run together end to end with demo data, but:

- container images are not published yet, so the installer only works with `--build` (build from source) or `--dry-run`;
- configuration, APIs and data can change in breaking ways until v1;
- it hasn't been load-tested or installed on a real server end to end yet.

Don't use it for your organization's real mail yet. Trying it locally and reporting problems helps a lot.

## What license is it under?

Suitehive is licensed under the [GNU Affero General Public License v3.0](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html) (AGPL-3.0). You can use, self-host, modify and share it freely. If you run a modified version as a service for others, you must publish your changes under the same license.

## What does it cost?

There are no per-seat fees. The only cost is the server you run it on (and your domain).

## What's included today?

Mail (web app, IMAP, SMTP), Calendar (web app, CalDAV, invitations) and one shared account with single sign-on, 2-step verification and an admin console. Contacts, Drive, Docs and Chat are planned; see the [Roadmap](/docs/roadmap/).

## Which mail and calendar apps work with it?

Suitehive uses open standards, so apps that speak them can connect:

| What | Protocol | Port |
|---|---|---|
| Reading mail | IMAP over TLS | 993 |
| Sending mail | SMTP submission | 465 (TLS) or 587 (STARTTLS) |
| Calendars | CalDAV | 443 |

Apps sign in with an **app password**, not your account password. See [Connecting apps](/docs/user-guide/connecting-apps/). Contacts sync (CardDAV) isn't available yet.

Calendar invitations are standard iCalendar email, so they work with people on other mail systems.

## What hardware do I need?

Measured at idle with demo data, the whole stack uses about 400 MB of RAM and 0.7 GB of disk before any mail is stored.

| Team size | CPU | RAM | Disk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trial, up to 10 users | 1 vCPU | 2 GB | 10 GB SSD |
| Up to 50 users | 2 vCPU | 4 GB | 40 GB SSD plus mail |
| Around 500 users (estimate) | 4 vCPU | 8 GB | 100 GB or more |

The installer expects about 4 GB of memory unless you pass `--skip-checks`. ClamAV antivirus adds 1 to 1.5 GB and rspamd about 300 MB. Mail fills the disk: plan for users times average mailbox size. See [Requirements](/docs/getting-started/requirements/).

## Do I need my own domain and server?

For real use, yes: a Linux server with a public IP address, Docker, and a domain where you can add DNS records. Ports 25, 80, 443, 465, 587 and 993 must be reachable, and outbound port 25 must be open (or use a [smarthost relay](/docs/self-hosting/smarthost/)). For a trial, you only need Docker on your laptop: see [Quick start](/docs/getting-started/quick-start/).

## Can I run it on Windows or macOS?

For a local trial, yes: macOS and Linux with `install.sh --local`, and Windows with Docker Desktop and `install.ps1`. Servers run Linux.

## Can I migrate from Google Workspace?

Not with a built-in tool yet. A Google Workspace import is on the roadmap. Today you can:

- import calendars from `.ics` files (see [Import and export](/docs/user-guide/calendar/import-export/));
- copy mail over IMAP with a third-party IMAP migration tool, since Suitehive Mail supports IMAP uploads.

## Can my other apps use "Sign in with Suitehive"?

Suitehive is an OpenID Connect provider, but registering your own applications isn't available yet. You can already let people sign in to Suitehive with Google or Microsoft; see [External sign-in](/docs/admin/external-sign-in/).

## Does Suitehive read or scan my mail?

There are no ads and no profiling. Mail checks incoming messages for SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and only scans for spam or viruses if you connect [rspamd or ClamAV](/docs/self-hosting/spam-and-antivirus/) on your own server. Remote images in messages are blocked until you allow them.

## How do I back it up?

`suitehive backup` writes one archive with the databases, stored mail and your configuration. See [Backups and restore](/docs/self-hosting/backups/).

## How do I report a security problem?

Report it privately to the maintainers instead of opening a public issue.
